Nothing here compounds. Each year and month cell is the sum of that period’s daily returns on the same $500k, and the balance chart is that sum in dollars — the convention the √-impact cost model is calibrated to. Compounding the same track would read like a number no desk could trade: from about year eight the balance is past the vol-premium leg’s vega capacity, low tens of millions. One useful consequence: under this convention every figure on the page scales linearly with the dial, so the charts below are drawn at the shipped 2× and any other rung is one multiplication away.

Sharpe and months-in-profit do not move with the dial at all — only the money and the pain do, in proportion. That is the whole trade-off: +79% a year costs a −30% drawdown and a −22% month at 2×.

Read this before the headline.
  • Nine of these fifteen years are not this book. Breakout and BAB list in 2020; before that this is vol premium plus the cross-sectional leg, which is why the early bars are taller and thinner evidence. Measured only where all four are live, 2020 onward, the book returns +54% a year against +79% for the full window, at Sharpe 2.76 instead of 3.50, worst month −13.7%. Plan against the 2020 number.
  • One leg is more than half the P&L. Vol premium is short variance: it earns steadily and loses suddenly. Its worst day ungated is −76.4%; the two regime gates it ships with take that to −10.0%, and across eight dislocations from 2008 to the 2025 tariff shock the gated leg loses more than 4% in exactly one — 2008, before the curve data the gate needs existed. That concentration is the real risk here, not the drawdown number above.
  • The dial is real, not free. 4.0× is on the table above because the arithmetic is linear, not because it is advisable: it turns a −30% drawdown into −60% of the capital, and a book that loses that much is a book that gets turned off before it recovers.
